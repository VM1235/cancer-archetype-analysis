Five datasets, one pipeline: the whole analysis as a workflow

Mouse skin SCC, Cook cell lines, mouse PDAC, 4T1 clones and MCF10A/HuMEC pseudospace (GSE114687). The method was built on SCC first, then re-run on the other four. Source: v2 report (17 Oct 2026).

Two questions asked of every dataset. Q1: do EMT "task" corners (a simplex) exist? Q2: do hybrid cells sit at a corner (specialists) or inside (generalists)?

1. The shared backbone

Every dataset runs the same sequence. Only the data-loading, labels and cleaning differ (section 2).

5. How the story converged

Q1 · Do task corners exist?

Partly, but not as epithelial / hybrid / mesenchymal.
  • EMT genes never give a simplex beyond sample identity (SCC, Cook, PDAC, 4T1).
  • Pooled corners are identity: tumours or assay, cell lines, mice, clones, samples.
  • Corners that survive are programmes: proliferation, TGF-β/matrix, hypoxia, differentiation.
  • Only GSE114687 gives a recurring triangle: cycling, differentiated, EMT/matrix.
  • EMT itself behaves like an axis, not a corner.

Q2 · Where do hybrids lie?

Mostly interior (generalists); in no dataset do they own a corner.
  • SCC 98.8%, 4T1 83–87%, PDAC early-hybrid 88% (closest 5%).
  • Cook: middle time point most generalist (89%).
  • GSE114687: hybrid is most generalist in 7 of 8 (gap), 8 of 8 (closest 5%).
  • Exception: HuMEC Mock under the gap rule, where epithelial cells are slightly more generalist.

Caveats that apply across all five

Most datasets have one sample per condition; EMT labels are partly derived from the score they are compared with (PDAC, 4T1, GSE114687); GSE114687 has no replicates; results flip direction with cleaning; none shows that EMT is or is not a Pareto trade-off in tumours.
